## Prototype objective

Create a coherent visual story that lets Aaron and later research participants experience:

1. how an independent instructor discovers and understands the product;
2. what a realistic golfer roadmap looks like;
3. how the instructor appears to configure the minimum identity and package information;
4. how the instructor appears to create, preview, and share the first roadmap;
5. what the golfer receives and how the package recommendation follows from the assessment; and
6. how the concept handles honest alternatives, errors, and seasonality without pretending to be a working product.

The prototype should feel complete enough to judge hierarchy, comprehension, credibility, personal relevance, trust, and flow. It should not be engineered to prove real persistence, delivery, billing, or account behavior.

## Fidelity and simulation contract

### What should feel real

- Content hierarchy and screen sequence.
- Pricing visibility and commercial boundaries.
- Required versus optional instructor inputs.
- The connection from coach input to golfer output.
- Preview parity with the golfer experience.
- Ethical package presentation and genuine non-purchase choices.
- Error, no-recommendation, share-failure, access, and seasonal-account explanations.
- Narrow and wide reading order.

### What must remain visibly simulated

- Account creation and sign-in.
- Form saving or persistence.
- Logo upload and link validation.
- Media playback or upload.
- Roadmap generation.
- Email, text, link delivery, or recipient verification.
- Purchase, booking, contact submission, or payment.
- Trial conversion, billing failure, pause, cancellation, or resume.
- Analytics, support response, or outcome measurement.

Use a persistent prototype note where ambiguity is likely:

> Visual prototype — nothing will be created, saved, sent, booked, paused, cancelled, or charged.

Do not place the note so prominently that it prevents realistic comprehension testing; show it before every simulated consequential action and in moderator setup.

## Click and transition rules

- Use instant or restrained dissolve transitions; no scroll-jacking, autoplay, parallax, countdown, confetti, or motion-only meaning.
- Preserve a visible back route on every sequential frame.
- Use hotspot labels in the Figma review page, not in participant mode.
- For preset form completion, the first click may move from an empty/prefilled frame to a completed state; never imply real persistence.
- Invalid-link, blocker, failed-share, pause, cancel, and unauthorized states must be deliberately reachable.
- Preview must show the same content/version as GOL-01 through GOL-08.
- Continue, ask, wait, and independent-practice choices must lead to different neutral outcomes.
- External action ends at a handoff preview stating that nothing was booked or charged.
- Prototype restart resets only the visual scenario.
