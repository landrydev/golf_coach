# Golfer Interview Guide

**CURRENT DRAFT STATUS:** Revised for Business Plan V2  
**RESEARCH STATUS:** No interviews completed  
**Depends on:** [Research Plan](RESEARCH_PLAN.md), [Ethical Sales and Trust Rules](../03_experience_strategy/ETHICAL_SALES_AND_TRUST_RULES.md)

## Research purpose

Understand how adult golfers experience assessments, coaching recommendations, packages, progress, renewal, and referral. Determine whether the proposed roadmap improves comprehension and confidence without creating pressure or false certainty.

Golfers are end users, not the initial software buyer. Do not ask them to evaluate the platform price.

## Target participant

- Adult recreational golfer.
- Completed an assessment or first coaching session recently enough to recall it.
- Include package purchasers, non-purchasers, and delayed decision-makers.
- Do not recruit junior golfers until safeguarding and consent requirements are resolved.

## Session setup

- 45–55 minutes for discovery; a separate versioned prototype session may be shorter.
- Obtain approved consent before notes or recording.
- Do not request identifiable coach records or sensitive media.
- Ask about real behavior before showing a concept.
- Separate participant statement, observed behavior, and moderator interpretation.

### Opening language

> We are studying how golfers understand what a coach recommends after an assessment or first lesson. This is not a test of you or your coach, and we are interested in confusing or uncomfortable moments as well as good ones.

## Discovery sequence

### 1. Goal and coaching context

- What were you hoping to change in your golf?
- Why did it matter at that time?
- What had you already tried?
- What made you choose an assessment or first lesson?

### 2. Most recent assessment

- Walk me through what happened from arrival to leaving.
- What did the coach learn about your goal?
- What observations, video, measurements, or drills were used?
- What did you understand about your strengths and main barriers?
- What was clear immediately, and what remained unclear?

### 3. Recommendation and package decision

- What did the coach recommend next?
- What did you think the first phase was meant to accomplish?
- What did the package include and cost?
- What information did you receive after the session?
- What did you do next: buy, ask, wait, decline, or something else?
- What made the choice easy or hard?
- Did any wording, timing, or interface feel pressuring?

### 4. Ongoing value and renewal

- During coaching, how did you know what mattered next?
- What evidence felt meaningful rather than decorative?
- What happened when progress was slow or reversed?
- How did you know a phase was complete?
- How was more coaching recommended?
- What would justify continuing, pausing, or stopping?

### 5. Sharing and referral

- Have you shared coaching progress with anyone? What and why?
- What should always remain private?
- When would a progress story feel earned?
- How should a referral request be separated from sharing?
- What control would you expect over audience, preview, correction, and withdrawal?

## Concept-exposed prototype sequence

Show only the assigned version of the sample or prototype. Do not explain the intended story before tasks.

### Task 1 — Orient

- Open the roadmap as if it came from your coach.
- Tell me whose plan it is, what it is for, and what you would do first.

### Task 2 — Retell the plan

- What is the golfer trying to achieve?
- What is the starting point?
- What are the most important barriers?
- What comes first, and why?
- Which future phases feel directional rather than promised?

### Task 3 — Evaluate the package

- What is being recommended?
- How does it connect to the first phase?
- What is included, and what remains unclear?
- Find how to continue, ask a question, wait, or decline.

### Task 4 — Evidence and trust

- Which statements are observations, interpretations, or plans?
- What limitations or uncertainty can you find?
- Does any element imply a guaranteed result?
- What feels personal beyond name, image, or colour?
- At any point did the experience itself make you feel pressured?

### Task 5 — Recovery and access

- Find what you would do if a link failed or information looked wrong.
- Use the assigned narrow-screen, limited-data, keyboard, zoom, or media-alternative condition.
- Explain what you expect about privacy and sharing.

## Comparative concept rule

When comparing a verbal recap, lesson-count offer, and roadmap, keep the underlying synthetic facts, package, and coach recommendation equivalent. Randomize order where practical. Ask for retell and action before preference.

## Evidence to record

- Unaided retell of goal, starting point, first phase, package purpose, and next action.
- Time and path to relevant information.
- Substantive personal details recognized.
- Misinterpretations, false certainty, and missing terms.
- Buy/ask/wait/decline behavior in the scenario.
- Trust, pressure, privacy, and sharing concerns.
- Assistance and recovery.
- Difference between stated preference and observable comprehension.

## Stop conditions

Stop for consent withdrawal, distress, unsafe identifiable disclosure, accidental real-client exposure, inaccessible task conditions that cannot be corrected, or a prototype that presents misleading real-world claims.

## Completion standard

Do not summarize a golfer as “positive” or “negative.” Enter discrete evidence in [EVIDENCE_LOG.md](EVIDENCE_LOG.md), including version, context, behavior, exact retell, assistance, counterevidence, and limitation.
