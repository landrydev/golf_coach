## Purpose

Validate whether a Canada-wide, self-serve product for individual golf instructors can create an ethical package-sale, renewal, and referral outcome at low acquisition, setup, and support cost.

This plan must distinguish problem evidence, concept evidence, assisted-beta learning, true self-serve behavior, and scaled commercial evidence. Founder help can reveal problems; it cannot prove the long-term model.

## Decisions this research must support

1. Is the independent instructor the right initial customer?
2. Is the assessment-to-roadmap problem frequent and costly enough to solve?
3. Can the product proposition be understood without a live explanation?
4. Can standardized templates preserve personal relevance and coach authority?
5. Can an instructor create and share the first roadmap without onboarding?
6. Does the golfer understand the plan and package recommendation without feeling pressured?
7. Will instructors make a real paid choice around **[PRICING HYPOTHESIS — REQUIRES VALIDATION] CAD $75 per month**?
8. Can acquisition and support economics plausibly fit that price?
9. What cancellation, pause, and next-season reactivation behavior should the model support?
10. Which lifecycle moments justify continued subscription?
11. Is a later team tier worth exploring without changing the solo first product?

## Participant profiles and samples

### Instructor discovery — 12 participants

- 10 core solo or individually controlled instructors.
- 2 small-team or facility boundary participants.
- More than one Canadian region; no single-city dependence.
- Outdoor/seasonal, mixed, and indoor/year-round contexts.
- Recent assessment and package-decision examples required.
- A mix of mature and informal package offers.

### Golfer discovery — 12 participants

- Adults who recently completed an assessment or first lesson.
- A mix of package purchasers, non-purchasers, and delayed decision-makers.
- Enough experience to describe the actual recommendation and follow-up.
- No junior participants until consent and safeguarding requirements are resolved.

### Unassisted acquisition and activation concept test — 8 instructors

- Previously unexposed to the proposition.
- Match the core ICP and have a plausible recent assessment scenario.
- Begin from a landing-page or direct-outreach link, not a founder explanation.
- Attempt sample interpretation, signup, setup, first-roadmap creation, preview, and share in the non-coded prototype.
- Moderator observes and probes after tasks; no task coaching.

### Golfer roadmap prototype — 10 golfers plus 5 instructor reviews

- Golfers complete unaided comprehension, next-action, trust, and recovery tasks.
- Instructors assess authenticity, coach ownership, effort, and package fit.
- Use the same synthetic facts across alternatives when comparing structure.

### Assisted beta — 5–8 instructors

Founder help is allowed to expose missing content, workflow, policy, and support needs. Each intervention is timestamped, categorized, and treated as a product gap. Assisted completion does not count toward human-free activation.

### True self-serve beta — at least 15 instructors

This stage occurs only after the design gate separately authorizes a limited product. No scheduled onboarding or founder-authored roadmaps. Reactive support, price response, real shares, continued use, cancellation, and reactivation are recorded.

Sample sizes are planning recommendations, not claims of statistical representativeness.

## Research stages and gates

| Stage | Artifact or behavior | What it can prove | What it cannot prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Problem discovery | Recent workflows and current artifacts | Problem frequency, current workaround, language, buying and seasonal context | Product desirability or payment |
| 2. Message and sample | Landing page, realistic sample roadmap, FAQ, price presentation | Unaided comprehension and credibility | Real activation or retention |
| 3. Activation prototype | Non-coded self-serve flow | Information architecture, task comprehension, friction and recovery | Production support economics or live sharing |
| 4. Golfer prototype | Roadmap and package decision | Comprehension, trust, personal relevance, action clarity | Commercial causality |
| 5. Assisted beta | Real instructor/golfer situations with help logged | Content sufficiency, workflow, risks, real package behavior | Scalable self-service |
| 6. True self-serve beta | Real product after explicit gate approval | Activation, support dependence, payment and early retention | Scaled CAC or complete seasonal retention unless cohort/time supports it |
| 7. Product-led acquisition cohorts | Channel-to-retention behavior | CAC, payback, conversion, referral contribution | Causality outside the defined cohort |

Do not advance merely because participants like the concept. Each gate uses predeclared observable behavior and records contrary evidence.

## Research methods

- Recent-behavior interviews with artifact walkthroughs.
- Workflow observation and task timing.
- First-click and unaided retell from the acquisition concept.
- Comparative sample-roadmap comprehension using equivalent facts.
- Non-coded task testing across narrow and wide layouts.
- Structured support-intervention logging.
- Actual paid choice where ethically and operationally authorized.
- Cohort measurement of activation, recurring use, cancellation, pause, reactivation, and referrals.

## Interview sequencing rule

Ask about the most recent real behavior before exposing the concept. Capture the current solution, cost, frequency, and consequences. Only then show the product. Pricing questions follow comprehension and concrete value evaluation, not a hypothetical enthusiasm rating.

## Evidence-quality standard

- Record behavior, direct participant language, artifact, context, date, and sample limitation.
- Distinguish observed action, participant report, moderator inference, and simulated expectation.
- Version every tested artifact.
- Record assistance, prompt, error, abandonment, and recovery—not only completion.
- Do not treat a researcher-scheduled demo as website comprehension.
- Do not treat an assisted roadmap as self-serve activation.
- Do not treat a stated willingness to pay as payment evidence.
- Segment seasonal from non-seasonal cancellation.
- Preserve negative and contradictory evidence in [EVIDENCE_LOG.md](EVIDENCE_LOG.md).

## Decision rules

### Support

A hypothesis can be provisionally supported only when the relevant behavioral threshold passes and no critical ethical, privacy, accessibility, or data-quality failure invalidates interpretation.

### Narrow or modify

Modify the segment, message, sequence, template, price, policy, or scope when the problem exists but a bounded friction prevents the intended behavior.

### Reject or pause

Pause when value requires founder explanation, custom implementation, recurring high support, facility procurement, pressure tactics, or economics incompatible with the working price.

## Stop conditions

Stop a session or study for consent withdrawal, participant distress, accidental identifiable data exposure, unsafe real sharing, misleading commercial representation, or an unresolved policy that makes the task inappropriate. Stop scaling if early evidence is being improved through increasingly intensive human assistance.
