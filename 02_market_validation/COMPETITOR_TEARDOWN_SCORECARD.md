# Competitor Teardown Scorecard

## Purpose

Evaluate both the golfer-facing roadmap job and the self-serve instructor acquisition/activation job. The relevant alternative may be another coaching product, a general document tool, a verbal recap, or a managed service—not only a direct category competitor.

No score may be filled from memory. Use current official primary sources, appropriately authorized trials or demos, and dated observations. A marketing claim is not evidence that the workflow succeeds.

## Priority review set

`[SIMULATED OWNER DECISION — REQUIRES AARON REVIEW]`

Review examples from four alternative groups:

1. golf coaching, player-development, or lesson-delivery products;
2. video, launch-monitor, booking, payment, or communication tools that already contain part of the workflow;
3. general document, presentation, form, or client-portal tools an instructor could configure independently; and
4. custom reports, templates, consultants, or founder-built services that compete with the perceived need for software.

Include at least one strong self-serve SaaS outside golf solely to study clear product explanation, sample output, pricing, signup, onboarding, cancellation, and reactivation patterns. Do not use it as market-demand evidence.

## Research scenarios

### Scenario A — prospect discovery

Arrive from search, content, community link, referral, partnership, or direct outreach. Determine whether a solo instructor can understand customer, outcome, required inputs, compatibility, price, and next step without requesting a call.

### Scenario B — first activation

Attempt account creation, basic brand setup, package setup, first-client input, output preview, sharing, error recovery, and return. Record every required human touch, upgrade block, custom-config step, and missing state.

### Scenario C — end-client decision

Assess whether the output connects goal, evidence, priorities, sequence, recommendation, package, terms, and unpressured next action.

### Scenario D — retention and seasonality

Locate cancellation, pause, data access, deletion, resume, annual billing, usage limits, and support expectations. Record what is visible before purchase versus only after account creation.

## Evidence collection method

For each observation record:

- product and exact page or in-product location;
- source URL or authorized trial context;
- access date and region/currency;
- plan/tier and signed-in state;
- exact observed behavior versus vendor claim;
- screenshot or note location where permitted;
- unknown, unavailable, or paywalled items;
- score rationale and confidence; and
- implication framed as hypothesis, not copied feature priority.

## Scoring scale

| Score | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Missing, blocked, or contradicts the criterion. |
| 1 | Present only as a claim or weak/manual path. |
| 2 | Partly observable but unclear, incomplete, or high-friction. |
| 3 | Clear and usable in the tested context. |
| 4 | Strong, coherent, and evidenced across the relevant path and states. |
| N/O | Not observable; never convert to zero. |
| N/A | Not applicable with reason. |

## Commercial-model flags

Record yes/no/unknown for:

- sales call required;
- custom quote required;
- mandatory setup fee;
- annual contract required;
- facility or multi-seat minimum;
- active-client usage pricing;
- paid onboarding or implementation;
- concierge report creation;
- easy cancellation;
- pause/read-only state;
- trial card requirement; and
- visible support boundary.

These are observations, not automatic reasons to copy or reject a model.

## Synthesis template

For each alternative summarize:

1. strongest observable self-serve pattern;
2. strongest end-client clarity pattern;
3. hidden effort or human-service dependency;
4. price and seasonality facts with currency/context;
5. ethical, privacy, or accessibility concern;
6. what the product should test rather than assume; and
7. whether the alternative already solves the full job for the initial customer.

## Decision rules

- Preserve differentiation only when instructor and golfer research confirms the job.
- Do not add features for scorecard parity.
- Stop claiming differentiation if an existing tool already performs the same job well for the core customer.
- Do not infer economical acquisition, support, or retention from a polished signup flow.
- Treat unavailable price or cancellation information as a transparency finding, not proof of high price.

## Completion criteria

The teardown is complete only when dated primary-source observations exist, unknowns remain marked, current prices are not generalized across regions or tiers, and all resulting recommendations are entered as hypotheses in the decision or evidence log.
