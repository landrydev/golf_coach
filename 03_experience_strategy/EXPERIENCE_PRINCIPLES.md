These nine principles are the candidate tradeoff rules for the acquisition, instructor, and golfer experiences.

## 1. Explain before asking

State audience, outcome, mechanism, required inputs, compatibility, price, and terms before asking for signup or payment.

- **Choose:** realistic sample output and transparent effort.
- **Reject:** gated demonstrations, vague benefit claims, custom-quote language, and hidden trial terms.
- **Test:** Can an unexposed instructor accurately retell what the product does before creating an account?

## 2. Move to real value, not dashboard activity

Guide the instructor to a complete, shareable first roadmap through the shortest credible path.

- **Choose:** readiness list, required/optional distinction, sensible examples, preview, and safe recovery.
- **Reject:** empty dashboards, extensive configuration, academy administration, and tutorial completion as activation.
- **Test:** Can the instructor create and share without human onboarding?

## 3. Begin with the golfer's meaning

Lead with the golfer's goal, reason, and context before technical findings or a purchase.

- **Choose:** the golfer's recognizable language.
- **Reject:** generic “improve your swing” framing or coach-defined priorities detached from the goal.
- **Test:** Does the golfer recognize their purpose before a metric, fault, phase, or price?

## 4. Curate evidence; do not display inventory

Show only evidence that helps explain a claim, significance, and limit.

- **Choose:** one representative clip, observation, comparison, or small measurement set with interpretation.
- **Reject:** feeds, raw-data dumps, equal-weight cards, and cherry-picked best results.
- **Test:** Can the golfer explain what evidence supports and does not prove?

## 5. Keep the coach visibly responsible

Every diagnosis, priority, phase, interpretation, and recommendation belongs to the coach.

- **Choose:** coach voice, rationale, confirmation, and restrained branding.
- **Reject:** coachless language, product-authored judgment, or a universal methodology.
- **Test:** Can the golfer identify who made the recommendation and why?

## 6. Give one thing priority

At any moment, one task, goal, phase, practice action, or next step should lead.

- **Choose:** progressive disclosure and a clear sequence.
- **Reject:** equal-weight card walls, option grids, drill libraries, and a complete dashboard before value.
- **Test:** Can the user answer “what matters now?” within ten seconds?

## 7. Earn every commercial action

Show meaning, evidence, reasoning, value, price, and relevant terms before purchase, renewal, sharing, or referral.

- **Choose:** one recommendation plus genuine ask, wait, decline, pause, or independent-practice paths.
- **Reject:** urgency, scarcity, guilt, fear, hidden alternatives, preselected paid states, or cancellation obstruction.
- **Test:** Is the experience still useful and respectful when no purchase occurs?

## 8. Tell the truth about progress and business value

Match language to evidence quality. Keep setbacks, uncertainty, assistance, support cost, and seasonal churn visible.

- **Choose:** explicit evidence labels and separate assisted versus self-serve reporting.
- **Reject:** invented milestones, guaranteed revenue, founder-assisted activation counted as product success, or seasonal cancellation hidden inside generic churn.
- **Test:** Can every material golfer and company claim be supported and qualified?

## 9. Protect control, privacy, access, and exit

Core meaning and legitimate actions must work privately, responsively, with accessible interaction, and with clear correction, cancellation, pause, and return expectations.

- **Choose:** private defaults, keyboard access, media alternatives, plain recovery, and simple account control.
- **Reject:** public defaults, color-only meaning, forced motion, inaccessible alternatives, hidden data retention, or cancellation friction.
- **Test:** Can users understand and control the experience without a precise pointer, sound, motion, public disclosure, or support call?
