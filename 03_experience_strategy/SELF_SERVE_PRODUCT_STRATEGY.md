## Strategic intent

`[SUPPORTED BY BUSINESS PLAN V2]`

Build a standardized, Canada-wide SaaS product that an individual golf instructor can discover, understand, adopt, configure, use, and cancel without relying on founder explanation or service delivery.

The product succeeds when self-service and golfer value reinforce each other:

```text
Clear product promise
→ credible sample
→ simple signup and setup
→ real first roadmap
→ golfer understands the package
→ instructor sees a commercial opportunity
→ paid and repeated use
→ progress, renewal, and referral value
```

Founder-led interviews, demonstrations, outreach, and assisted beta work remain useful research methods. They are not the intended operating model.

## Product-led acquisition journey

```text
Awareness
→ landing-page explanation
→ realistic sample golfer roadmap
→ price and FAQ
→ signup or trial
→ instructor setup
→ first roadmap created
→ first roadmap shared
→ first package opportunity
→ paid subscription
→ continued use
→ instructor referral
```

Every transition needs a measurable user action and an honest exit. A lead does not become “activated” because the founder demonstrated the product; a signup does not become “value” until a credible roadmap reaches a real golfer.

## Acquisition-page job

The minimum acquisition experience must answer:

1. **What problem is this for?** A strong coaching assessment often becomes an unclear verbal or fragmented package recommendation.
2. **What commercial outcome does it support?** More appropriate package commitments, renewals, and referrals through clarity and trust.
3. **What is the product?** A coach-branded golfer development roadmap and player journey.
4. **How does it work?** Coach inputs become a structured golfer-facing story.
5. **What must the instructor provide?** Identity, package, golfer goal, concise assessment, barriers, phases, and recommendation.
6. **What stays in place?** Coaching methods, launch monitor, video, booking, payment, and communication tools.
7. **What does the golfer receive?** A private, personal roadmap with a clear first phase and next action.
8. **What does it cost?** **[PRICING HYPOTHESIS — REQUIRES VALIDATION] CAD $75 per month per instructor.**
9. **Can I try it without a call?** Candidate self-serve trial or direct purchase, with price visible before signup.
10. **Can I leave?** Month-to-month cancellation and a candidate seasonal pause are explained plainly.

## Self-serve activation strategy

### Before beginning

Show a concise readiness list:

- coach name and optional logo/accent;
- one current lesson package and its existing next-action link;
- one real golfer's goal and assessment;
- two or three priority barriers; and
- a rough phase sequence.

Make clear that video and measurements are optional and existing tools remain in place.

### Guided activation

The 12-step experience is specified in [SELF_SERVE_INSTRUCTOR_ACTIVATION.md](../04_wireframes/SELF_SERVE_INSTRUCTOR_ACTIVATION.md): promise, sample, signup, identity, package, first golfer, assessment, phases, preview, share, success, and return.

At every step:

- ask only for information necessary now;
- explain why an input matters to the golfer;
- show an example without prescribing coaching judgment;
- save progress and support safe return;
- make optional fields visibly optional;
- provide specific error recovery;
- allow preview before share; and
- avoid support-dependent dead ends.

### Activation event

`[SIMULATED OWNER DECISION — REQUIRES AARON REVIEW]`

> An instructor creates and shares a complete golfer roadmap connected to a current lesson package, without human onboarding.

Supporting milestones are account created, identity complete, package complete, first golfer started, first draft complete, first preview, and first share. Only the final event demonstrates that the first value crossed from setup to real use.

## Trial and conversion strategy

`[SIMULATED OWNER DECISION — REQUIRES AARON REVIEW]` Test a 14-day no-card trial that provides the complete activation loop. Show **[PRICING HYPOTHESIS — REQUIRES VALIDATION] CAD $75/month** before account creation, state what happens at the end, and require an explicit paid choice.

Risks to test:

- an instructor may not have a real assessment during 14 days;
- no-card trials may attract unqualified curiosity;
- asking for payment before the first share may interrupt activation;
- asking after the share may delay learning about willingness to pay; and
- a trial reminder can become pressure if it obscures access or deletion behavior.

Compare trial behavior with a sample-first direct monthly purchase only after consistent eligibility and activation definitions exist.

## Recurring value and retention

The instructor should continue paying because the product supports repeated real roadmaps and, when validated, makes ongoing progress, phase completion, renewal, and referral moments easier to deliver.

The return state should make these next jobs obvious without becoming a complete dashboard:

- create another roadmap;
- find a recently shared roadmap;
- correct or update a roadmap;
- see what still needs review;
- later, add meaningful progress or complete a phase; and
- understand account status, cancellation, pause, or resume.

`[UNVALIDATED BUSINESS ASSUMPTION]` First-share success alone may not sustain a subscription. Research must identify the natural frequency of new assessments and the later deliverables that produce recurring value.

## Seasonality strategy

`[SIMULATED OWNER DECISION — REQUIRES AARON REVIEW]` Use month-to-month service at **[PRICING HYPOTHESIS — REQUIRES VALIDATION] CAD $75/month**, cancel anytime, with a candidate read-only seasonal pause at **[PRICING HYPOTHESIS — REQUIRES VALIDATION] CAD $15/month** for up to six months. Provide a clear explanation before purchase and a low-friction resume state.

Design questions requiring validation:

- which information remains available during pause;
- whether sharing links remain usable;
- how data retention differs after cancellation;
- when reminders are useful rather than intrusive;
- how year-round instructors experience the policy;
- how to distinguish seasonal cancellation from dissatisfaction; and
- whether annual billing becomes useful only after a complete seasonal cohort.

No annual contract is required for launch.

## Low-support operating model

Support must be treated as a product metric, not a courtesy outside the economics.

### Product-provided help

- readiness checklist;
- small examples beside difficult inputs;
- clear optional/deferred labels;
- preview and editable review summary;
- inline validation with specific recovery;
- autosave/save-and-return expectation in the future design;
- FAQ and concise help;
- clear contact route for reactive support; and
- honest limits rather than a promise that every workflow fits.

### Human-support boundaries

Normal service does not include custom roadmap writing, brand design, package consulting, scheduled onboarding, data cleanup, facility configuration, or recurring performance reporting.

`[SIMULATED OWNER DECISION — REQUIRES AARON REVIEW]` Target median post-activation normal support of no more than 10 minutes per active account per month and at least 80% of activated instructors reaching value without human onboarding. These thresholds require real measurement.

## First product versus later team tier

### First product

- One instructor identity.
- Standard basic branding.
- Standard package setup.
- Solo roadmap authoring, preview, sharing, and return.
- Existing external action links.
- Candidate subscription, cancellation, pause, and resume states.

### Later team-tier discovery

- Multiple coach identities or seats.
- Shared branding or templates.
- Roles, permissions, or aggregate administration.
- Team billing or usage.

Team needs remain observations and future hypotheses. Do not add them to the first flow for a single facility prospect.

## Strategic refusal rules

Reject or explicitly revisit a request that makes the model depend on:

- a sales call or custom proposal;
- manual implementation;
- mandatory onboarding;
- a large setup fee;
- bespoke templates or custom branding;
- ongoing concierge service;
- facility procurement or a multi-seat minimum;
- uneconomical paid acquisition;
- hidden support labor;
- forced twelve-month payment; or
- product-authored coaching judgment.
