## Instructor value proposition

`[SIMULATED OWNER DECISION — REQUIRES AARON REVIEW]`

> **Sell the plan, not another hour.** Turn an assessment into a personalized, coach-branded development roadmap that helps the golfer understand why a structured first phase is worth considering—using the coaching evidence and tools you already have, through a product you can start without a sales call.

### Functional value

- Connect the golfer's goal, starting point, barriers, phase purpose, and package.
- Reuse selected observations, videos, drills, measurements, and on-course reports.
- Produce one coherent artifact to preview and share after an assessment.
- Use standardized prompts without surrendering coach voice or methodology.
- Preserve the instructor's brand, package, price, and existing next-action route.
- Begin, cancel, pause, or later reactivate without custom implementation.

### Commercial value

- Reduce uncertainty at the assessment-to-package decision.
- Improve the chance that a golfer chooses an appropriate structured phase.
- Keep the package connected to its purpose during delivery.
- Make phase completion the evidence base for renewal.
- Create earned, private-by-default referral moments.

These are intended outcomes, not guaranteed results.

### Emotional value for the instructor

- Confidence that the client experience reflects the quality of the coaching.
- Pride in a clear and deliberate presentation.
- Less dependence on repeating the whole plan from memory.
- Control over judgment, language, brand, and recommendation.
- Confidence that trying the product does not create a consulting engagement or long contract.

## Golfer value proposition

`[SIMULATED OWNER DECISION — REQUIRES AARON REVIEW]`

> **Know where you are, where you want to go, what comes first, and why your coach recommends this phase—before you decide whether to buy.**

The golfer should receive:

- their goal in recognizable language;
- an honest starting point that includes strengths;
- a small number of relevant barriers rather than a catalogue of faults;
- a directional development sequence with uncertainty acknowledged;
- a clear first-phase purpose and realistic progress signals;
- one relevant package with visible inclusions and terms; and
- an easy next action plus a visible way to ask, wait, or decline.

## Strategic positioning

`[SUPPORTED BY BUSINESS PLAN V2]`

The company is the presentation and revenue layer between coaching work and the golfer's buying experience. Video tools capture swings. Launch monitors produce measurements. Booking and payment systems complete transactions. Communication products carry messages. This product curates the most decision-relevant outputs into a personal development story.

Working positioning statement:

> For independent golf instructors whose coaching is more valuable than their follow-up communicates, the coach-branded development roadmap turns an assessment into a clear first-phase recommendation. Unlike an operations platform, data report, or custom design service, it makes the coach's reasoning easier to understand and purchase without changing the coach's method or current tools.

## Why self-service is part of the value

`[SUPPORTED BY BUSINESS PLAN V2]`

The business proposition includes the adoption experience. An instructor should be able to see a realistic sample, understand the work required, view the price, sign up, set identity and package information, create and share a first roadmap, and return later without relying on the founder.

The product is weakened if its value becomes clear only after a live explanation or if personalization requires bespoke setup.

## Objections and honest responses

| Objection | Product answer to test | What must not be claimed |
|---|---|---|
| “My verbal close already works.” | Use a real baseline and test consistency, client clarity, renewal, or referral value only if there is a gap. | Do not imply every coach sells badly. |
| “This will take too long.” | Show required inputs, time an unassisted real task, and defer nonessential fields. | Do not promise zero effort. |
| “It will feel generic.” | Let the coach own goal, rationale, priorities, phase names, evidence, and voice within a standard structure. | Do not claim a template alone creates personalization. |
| “My current platform already does this.” | Compare the complete golfer decision story; stop if the existing product performs the job well. | Do not misrepresent competitors. |
| “I do not want to pressure golfers.” | Make the rationale and alternatives clear; measure pressure and trust. | Do not hide sales intent or use urgency. |
| “I am seasonal.” | Explain cancel and candidate pause behavior transparently; measure reactivation. | Do not assume twelve paid months. |
| “I need help setting it up.” | Provide contextual examples, preview, defaults, and recovery. Measure all support. | Do not quietly convert onboarding into a mandatory service. |

## Credibility conditions

The offer is credible only when:

- the instructor authored or approved every coaching judgment;
- the golfer recognizes real goals and context;
- evidence labels and limits are visible;
- the package purpose is more prominent than lesson count;
- platform price, inclusions, cancellation, and seasonal terms are clear;
- the process works without custom implementation;
- normal support is low and measurable; and
- commercial claims are supported by behavior rather than aesthetics.

## Explicit non-promises

`[SUPPORTED BY BUSINESS PLAN V2]`

No guarantee of scoring, handicap, swing, package conversion, renewal, referral, or revenue; no universal methodology; no automated diagnosis; no fixed improvement timeline; no replacement of the instructor's operating tools; no promise of concierge service.
