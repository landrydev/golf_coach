## Recommended direction

`[SIMULATED OWNER DECISION — REQUIRES AARON REVIEW]`

Use one visual system with two related modes:

- **Instructor acquisition and activation:** **Calm Product Utility**—clear, direct, credible, transparent about work and price, and optimized for task completion.
- **Golfer roadmap and journey:** **Quiet Editorial Performance Portfolio**—private, coach-curated, personal, evidence-aware, and narratively paced.

The modes share brand foundations, accessibility rules, typography roles, color discipline, and coach identity. They differ in hierarchy and density because a prospect completing setup has a different job from a golfer reading a development story.

## Why two modes are necessary

The golfer experience should feel like a thoughtful professional review, not software operations. The instructor experience must make a low-cost self-serve product immediately understandable, not resemble a bespoke consulting engagement or luxury service requiring a sales call.

Using only the editorial mode risks hiding price, requirements, errors, and setup progress. Using only utility UI risks turning the golfer story into an impersonal dashboard. A shared system with deliberate modes preserves both clarity and emotional quality.

## Instructor mode — Calm Product Utility

### Mood

- Practical and composed.
- Lightweight but not flimsy.
- Friendly without conversational clutter.
- Transparent rather than sales-polished.
- Standardized without looking generic.

### Composition

- One primary message or task per region.
- Strong vertical explanation: problem → outcome → mechanism → sample → price → start.
- Forms use a single clear input column with contextual help adjacent only when space permits.
- Required, optional, saved, incomplete, error, trial, paid, pause, and cancel states are visible in text.
- Preview is visually separated from input but never treated as a custom-design canvas.
- The minimal return state uses recent work and one contextual action, not a dense dashboard.

### Commercial presentation

- Show **[PRICING HYPOTHESIS — REQUIRES VALIDATION] CAD $75/month per instructor** in plain, stable typography.
- Show inclusion, no setup fee, trial behavior, cancellation, and candidate pause near the decision.
- Avoid enterprise tables, “contact sales,” luxury exclusivity, crossed-out invented anchors, countdowns, and aggressive contrast around the CTA.
- A sample output should do more persuasive work than feature-icon rows.

## Golfer mode — Quiet Editorial Performance Portfolio

### Mood

- Personal and private.
- Warm but evidence-disciplined.
- Calm, confident, and honest about uncertainty.
- Editorial rather than card-dashboard based.
- Coach-led rather than platform-led.

### Composition

- Goal and coach note lead.
- A narrative spine moves through starting point, barriers, phases, first recommendation, package, and next action.
- One evidence item or coach note may sit beside its interpretation on wider screens; avoid grids of equal-weight metrics.
- White space separates narrative chapters and decisions.
- Package content feels like the practical conclusion to the plan, not an advertisement inserted into it.

## Shared typography personality

`[SIMULATED OWNER DECISION — REQUIRES AARON REVIEW]`

- A highly legible sans-serif should carry navigation, labels, forms, pricing, statuses, terms, evidence labels, and functional copy.
- An editorial serif may be explored for golfer-facing display headings and meaningful quotations only when it remains legible across widths, languages, and coach themes.
- Instructor input and golfer output must never depend on a decorative type choice to communicate hierarchy.
- Use aligned numerals where pricing or measurements benefit, without resembling financial software.

No typeface is selected by this plan. Licensing, availability, rendering, language support, and accessibility require Figma and specialist review.

## Information density and spacing

- Acquisition: generous message sections, concise proof, and a complete price/terms block.
- Activation: compact enough to show context, spacious enough to prevent field and error confusion.
- Preview: use the golfer reading rhythm, not form density.
- Golfer story: one narrative chapter per viewport region when practical; later phases become progressively lighter.
- Account states: consequences and actions stay together.
- Never use spacing to hide a legitimate alternative or make cancellation secondary to continuation.

## Color philosophy

- Use warm neutral or quietly cool surfaces with strong ink contrast.
- Reserve one platform accent for action/focus and allow a constrained coach accent only when contrast survives.
- Use semantic status colors only with text and shape/icon support.
- Price, required fields, evidence maturity, error, phase state, share selection, and account state never rely on color alone.
- Protect focus, error, success, privacy, and text roles from unsafe coach-brand overrides.

Candidate palette values belong in Figma exploration and are not production tokens or contrast-approved assets.

## Photography, video, and sample evidence

- Show credible coaching context rather than stock luxury-golf aspiration.
- Label the acquisition sample synthetic and realistic about optional media.
- Golfer media supports a coach claim; it is not a cinematic hero layer.
- Use consistent treatment for before/current comparisons.
- Cropping preserves relevant swing or coaching information.
- Every meaningful image/video has an equivalent text path.
- The product remains credible with no media.

## Shape, surface, icon, and data language

- Prefer modest radii, restrained borders, and minimal elevation.
- Use reading surfaces and clear form groups rather than a sea of cards.
- Phase sequences may use a line, numbering, or chapter rhythm; status remains explicit in text.
- Icons clarify familiar actions or states but never replace labels for price, errors, privacy, share, cancel, or pause.
- Avoid golf clichés unless tied meaningfully to content.
- Use a visualization only when it answers a golfer question more clearly than prose; pair it with interpretation, context, limitation, and a text equivalent.
- Do not place business analytics in the first instructor return state merely because data might later exist.

## Motion

- Motion can orient between steps or reveal sequence, but cannot carry meaning alone.
- No autoplay, parallax, scroll-jacking, countdown, confetti, or celebration tied to payment.
- Share success should feel grounded, not gamified.
- Reduced-motion and static versions retain full order and meaning.

## Coach and platform branding

- Golfer-facing content leads with coach identity; platform attribution is secondary.
- Instructor acquisition identifies the SaaS product clearly so subscription terms are not confused with the coach's package.
- Standard basic branding means name, identity/contact, optional logo/photo, and constrained accent—not custom page design.
- Test at least three fictional coach themes for contrast and hierarchy using identical content.

## Mobile-first behavior

- The Canada-wide self-serve experience must be understandable and operable on a narrow screen even if some instructors prefer wider authoring.
- Preserve landing explanation, sample, price, and CTA order.
- Forms remain one column; help follows the related field.
- Preview uses the golfer narrative order.
- Sticky actions never obscure terms, errors, focus, or account consequences.
- The golfer can understand goal, phase, package, and alternatives without horizontal scrolling.

## Moodboard collection plan

Collect references for properties, not imitation:

1. **Clear self-serve SaaS onboarding:** direct explanation, good examples, transparent state and recovery; reject enterprise demo funnels.
2. **Professional portfolios and reviews:** calm authority, specific evidence, narrative rhythm; reject luxury signaling.
3. **Coaching notebooks and plans:** human prioritization; reject decorative faux-handwriting.
4. **Long-form sports journalism:** personal story and documentary media; reject news-site density.
5. **Accessible forms and accounts:** clear labels, errors, save/return, price, cancel, pause, resume; reject hidden consequences.

Record source, date, property, rejection boundary, and license/usage status. Do not paste unlicensed assets into distributable artifacts.

## Visual anti-patterns

- Enterprise “book a demo” aesthetics.
- Premium-price or exclusivity cues that contradict a low-cost solo plan.
- Feature matrices implying multiple launch tiers.
- Empty dashboard before first value.
- Custom-brand controls suggesting a website builder.
- Bright conversion funnels or aggressive CTA repetition.
- Card walls, dense analytics, and raw launch-monitor styling.
- Golf ornament without meaning.
- Generic name/logo personalization.
- Hidden terms, low-contrast exits, or decorative errors.
- Confetti for signup, share, payment, or renewal.
- Coachless or autonomous-system imagery.

## Direction acceptance questions

- Can an unexposed instructor identify audience, outcome, required work, compatibility, price, and first action?
- Does the sample feel achievable through standard setup rather than custom service?
- Can an instructor complete forms and recover without assistance?
- Does the golfer experience feel personal because of content and coach judgment, not decoration?
- Are price, terms, alternatives, evidence limits, privacy, cancellation, and pause legible?
- Do both modes feel related without collapsing into a generic dashboard?
- Does every coach theme preserve hierarchy and accessibility?

`[REAL-WORLD VALIDATION REQUIRED]` Create two refinements within each mode using identical content, then test comprehension, trust, credible effort, personal relevance, and task success. Preference alone is insufficient.
