# Instructor beta research script

Use this with 5–8 adult independent instructors. Do not explain the product before the unaided comprehension section.

## Session record

Record participant ID, context, artifact version, assistance minutes, task completion, errors, time to first published roadmap, stated value, price response, and exact limitations. Do not store real golfer-identifying information in the research notes.

## Part 1 — Unaided proposition

1. Open the landing page.
2. Ask: “What do you think this product does, who is it for, and what would you expect to do first?”
3. Record the response before answering questions.

## Part 2 — First roadmap

Ask the instructor to register, configure a coach profile, add a package, create a synthetic golfer, build a roadmap, preview it, and publish it. Do not guide them unless they are fully blocked; record every intervention.

Observe:

- time to first publish;
- fields they skip or misunderstand;
- whether three/four phases feel natural;
- whether the result feels coach-owned and personal;
- whether the instructor trusts the private-link behaviour;
- whether they can find the next action after interruption.

## Part 3 — Ongoing value

Ask them to add a lesson, practice assignment, evidence item, and phase review. Ask which of these they would actually maintain after each lesson and which feel unnecessary.

## Part 4 — Golfer comprehension

Show the private plan to an adult golfer or have the instructor review it as a golfer. Ask the viewer to explain the goal, current priority, sequence, evidence limits, package relationship, and next action in their own words.

## Part 5 — Commercial response

Only after product use, show the proposed pricing hypothesis. Separate “sounds reasonable” from an actual bounded commitment. Ask what event would cause them to subscribe, continue, pause, or cancel.

## Decision rule

Do not add more major features until the evidence explains why the current beta cannot create or retain value. Prioritize repeated comprehension, authoring-time, trust, and recurring-use problems over individual feature requests.
